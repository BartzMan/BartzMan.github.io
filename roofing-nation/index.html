The work

What a roof needs, in order.

Four kinds of roofing work, from a full tear-off down to the deck to a single lifted shingle. Each one starts with someone on the roof looking at it.

01

Roof replacement

A full tear-off down to the deck. Soft decking comes out, new underlayment goes down, then shingles, ridge, vents and flashing go on in the right order.

  • Old roofing stripped, deck checked board by board
  • Underlayment, starter course, shingles, ridge cap
  • Vents, pipe boots and flashing replaced, not reused
Ask about a replacement
02

Low-slope & rolled roofing

Flat and low-pitch sections over porches, additions and garages need a different system than the main roof: rolled roofing over a proper base, so water moves off instead of pooling.

  • Base sheet and rolled cap sheet, seams lapped the right way
  • Tie-ins where the flat section meets the shingle slope
  • Drip edge and terminations sealed
Ask about a low-slope roof
03

Roof repair

Lifted or missing shingles, leaks at vents and pipe boots, flashing that has pulled away from a chimney or wall, and soft spots that show up after a storm.

  • Shingles matched and woven in, not just patched over
  • Chimney and wall flashing re-set and sealed
  • Pipe boots and vents swapped where they have cracked
Ask about a repair
04

Storm checks

After hail or a wind event, a walk over the whole roof: shingles, ridge, flashing, vents and edges, with photos of anything that needs attention and a plain explanation of what it means.

  • Every slope walked, not just the one you can see
  • Photos of bruised shingles, lifted tabs and bent vents
  • A written scope if work is needed, nothing if it is not
Ask about a storm check

Layer by layer

From the deck up to the ridge.

A shingle roof is a system, not just the part you see from the street. This is the order the layers go on during a replacement, and why each one matters in Gulf Coast heat and rain.

  1. 1DeckThe plywood or plank base. Soft or rotted sections are cut out and replaced first.
  2. 2Drip edgeMetal at the eaves that steers water into the gutter, not behind the fascia.
  3. 3UnderlaymentA water-shedding layer over the deck, lapped so water runs over each seam.
  4. 4Starter courseThe first row at the eave, sealing the roof edge down against wind lift.
  5. 5ShinglesCourses staggered up the slope, each covering the nails of the row below.
  6. 6Flashing & pipe bootsMetal at walls and chimneys, collars at pipes: where most leaks begin.
  7. 7Ridge cap & ventsCaps the top line and lets hot attic air out in a Deer Park summer.
From the roof

Field notes, from the ridge.

Job photos from Roofing Nation Renovation, taken up on the roof and from the air, plus what to look for from your own yard.

Read your roof
Ridge
The top line where two slopes meet.
Hip
An outside corner running down from the ridge.
Valley
An inside corner that channels water.
Eave
The low edge, where water leaves the roof.
Before you call

What to look for from the ground

  1. Shingle pieces or granules in the gutters and flower beds after a storm.
  2. Dark streaks or a dip in the roofline when you look from the street.
  3. Water stains on a ceiling, especially near a vent, chimney or wall.
  4. Flashing that has lifted, or rust at the edge of the roof.
