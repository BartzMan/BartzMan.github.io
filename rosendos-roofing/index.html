Roof work

What goes on top.

Shingle roofs on one- and two-story homes, the low porch roofs that are too flat for shingles, and the small parts that let water in when they wear out.

  1. 01

    Roof replacement

    Full tear-off down to the deck, new decking where the wood is soft, underlayment, then a new shingle roof up to the ridge.

    Tear-off · decking · shingles · ridge

    • Old roofing and soft decking out
    • New underlayment and metal drip edge
    • Shingles, vents and ridge cap on
  2. 02

    Roof repair

    Lifted or missing shingles, leaks around pipe boots and vents, and worn flashing along walls and chimneys.

    Shingles · flashing · pipe boots

    • Lifted, cracked or missing tabs
    • Leaks at pipe boots and vents
    • Flashing along walls and chimneys
  3. 03

    Flat & low-slope roofs

    Porch and patio roofs that are too flat for shingles get a rolled roofing membrane with a metal edge along the drip line.

    Porches · patio roofs · additions

    • Porch and patio roofs
    • Low additions and carports
    • Metal edge at the drip line
  4. 04

    Vents, boots & ridge

    Roof vents, pipe boots and ridge caps replaced so the attic can breathe and the seams stay sealed.

    Ventilation · ridge caps · seals

    • Box vents and ridge vents
    • Pipe boots and their seals
    • Ridge caps along the peak

How a re-roof goes on

Laid down in courses.

A roof is a stack of layers, and every layer depends on the one under it. This is the order the work happens in, from the first look to the last ridge cap.

  1. Look it over

    A walk around the house and a look at the shingles, the decking edges, the vents and the flashing.

  2. Tear-off

    Old shingles and underlayment come off, down to the wood deck, so nothing is hidden under the new roof.

  3. Deck & dry-in

    Soft or damaged decking is replaced with new panels, then the roof is dried in with underlayment and a metal drip edge.

  4. Shingles, course by course

    A starter strip at the eaves, then each course is laid and nailed up the slope, cut in around vents and walls.

  5. Ridge, vents & clean-up

    Ridge cap and vents go on last, then old roofing and loose nails are cleared from the yard and drive.

Roof layers

What's under the shingles.

The shingles are the part you see from the street. Here is everything else a new roof is built from, in the order it goes on.

  1. 1

    Rafters

    The roof's frame. Checked from the deck edge when the old roof is off.

  2. 2

    Decking

    Wood panels the whole roof is nailed to. Soft spots are cut out and replaced.

  3. 3

    Underlayment

    A water-shedding sheet laid in overlapping rows from the eave up.

  4. 4

    Drip edge

    Metal along the eaves that carries water past the fascia.

  5. 5

    Starter course

    The first row at the eave that seals the edge of the first shingle course.

  6. 6

    Shingles

    Laid in staggered courses so every seam is covered by the row above.

  7. 7

    Ridge cap

    Folded caps that close the peak where two slopes meet.

  8. 8

    Roof vents

    Let attic heat out, which matters on a Gulf Coast summer roof.

Before you call

Three things to have ready.

None of these are required, but they make the first conversation about your roof a lot shorter.

01

Photos from the ground

A few phone photos of the roof from the yard or the street, especially of anything that looks lifted, missing or stained.

02

Where the water shows

Stains on a ceiling or wall, which room, and whether it shows after every rain or only with wind from one side.

03

Your ZIP & roof type

Your ZIP code, one or two stories, and shingle or flat roof. That is enough to start talking about the job.
