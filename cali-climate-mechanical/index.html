1

The attic

The roof soaks up the afternoon sun and the air under it gets very hot. If the ceiling below is thinly insulated, that heat leaks into the rooms.

What usually helps: more attic insulation and sealing the gaps around the ceiling.

2

The ducts

Cool air runs through ducts that often sit in that hot attic. Leaky or thin-skinned ducts warm it up, or lose it, before it reaches a room.

What usually helps: sealing and insulating the ducts, or replacing sections that are old or damaged.

3

Upstairs rooms

Heat rises and the upstairs sits right under the attic, so it tends to run warmer than downstairs on hot days.

What usually helps: balancing the airflow, better returns, or a separate control for each floor.

4

West-facing windows

Late-day sun comes straight through west glass and heats the room fast, just when the outside air is hottest too.

What usually helps: shades, awnings or window film, so the air conditioner has less to fight.

5

The outdoor unit

The condenser pushes heat out of the house. It works best with clear space around it and clean coils.

What usually helps: keeping leaves, weeds and clutter away from the unit, and having a technician look it over if airflow seems weak.

Good to know

Why your upstairs runs hotter than downstairs.

Four common reasons, and what usually helps. Tap each one to light it up on the house.

Ducts in a hot attic

Cool air travels through ducts sitting in a very hot attic, so it arrives warmer than it left.

What usually helps: sealed, well-insulated ducts keep the cool air cool.

Not enough return air

If air cannot easily get back to the system from upstairs, the rooms feel stuffy and the airflow falls out of balance.

What usually helps: another return, or a clear path for the air to travel back.

Thin attic insulation

Heat from the roof leaks down through the ceiling into the rooms right beneath it.

What usually helps: more insulation over the upstairs ceiling.

One thermostat, two floors

Downstairs reaches the target first and the system shuts off while upstairs is still warm.

What usually helps: zoning, so each floor gets its own control.
