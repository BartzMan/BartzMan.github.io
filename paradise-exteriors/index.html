Impact windows

Glass made to stay in the frame.

What makes a window an impact window is the glass. It is laminated: two layers of glass laminated to a tough clear interlayer. If something hits it, the glass can crack, but the interlayer is designed to hold the pieces together in a heavy frame instead of leaving an open hole in the wall.

Storm prep

Storm-season questions.

vs

Impact glass: the protection is the window itself

Q1Shutters or impact windows: what is the difference?

Shutters go up over ordinary windows before a storm: panels, accordions or roll-downs that someone has to close. Impact windows use laminated glass in a heavy frame, so the protection is part of the window and there is nothing to put up. Which suits a house depends on the openings, the budget and how the home is used, so it is worth talking through.

Q2Should my roof be looked at after a storm?

After strong wind it is worth checking for lifted or missing shingles or tiles, bent flashing or drip edge, debris on the roof and new stains on ceilings. Stay off the roof yourself: photos taken from the ground are enough to start. Text them to (727) 291-0810.

Q3Do roof, window and door jobs need a permit?

In Florida, replacing a roof, windows or exterior doors usually needs a permit from the city or county. Permit questions are worked out job by job, as part of planning the work.

Q4When is hurricane season?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. Many homeowners plan roof, window and door work well before the busiest weeks, so it helps to get in touch early.

Q5What should I send when I text?

Photos of the roof, windows or doors you want done, your city or ZIP code, and roughly how many windows or doors are involved. Text them to (727) 291-0810.

Q6Do you work in Tampa too?

Yes. Paradise Exteriors works in St. Petersburg, Tampa and the surrounding areas. Say which city you are in when you get in touch.
