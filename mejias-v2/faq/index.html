Frequently asked questions

What areas do you serve?
We're based in Mesa, AZ (85203) and work in Mesa. If you're nearby, call or text and we'll tell you whether we can get to you.
How do I get an estimate?
Call or text (480) 969-6719 with a photo of the tree and a line about what you want. We'll take a look and give you a straightforward price.
Do you haul away the branches and debris?
Yes. Cleanup is part of the job, and reviewers regularly mention it. We take the limbs with us.
Do you remove large trees?
Yes. We handle large trees that need major equipment. Send a photo and a rough height and we'll tell you how we'd approach it.
Do you trim palm trees?
Yes. We remove dead fronds and seed pods, clean up shaggy trunks where it makes sense and haul everything away. We'll talk through how much comes off before we start.
Can you help after a monsoon storm?
Yes. We remove uprooted trees, clear fallen and hanging limbs and haul it away. Call or text a photo and we'll tell you how soon we can get there. If anyone is in danger or power lines are involved, call 911 or the utility first.
When is the best time to trim trees and palms in Mesa?
Many Arizona pros recommend late spring to early summer (roughly May to June) for palms and dense canopies like mesquite, ahead of monsoon season. Some deciduous trees are trimmed in winter. Timing depends on the species, so ask us about yours.
Do I need a permit to trim or remove a tree in Mesa?
Routine trimming on your own property usually doesn't. Removals can differ: trees in the public right-of-way and certain protected native trees may need a permit. Check with the City of Mesa if you're unsure.
Can you trim a tree that touches power lines?
No. Trees growing into or touching utility lines are a job for the utility company or a certified line-clearance crew. Stay well clear and call the utility.
How quickly can you come out?
Reach out and we'll give you a real date. If it's an urgent hazard or storm damage, tell us that up front.
How do I contact you?
Call or text (480) 969-6719. Texting a photo of the tree is the fastest way to get a straight answer.
