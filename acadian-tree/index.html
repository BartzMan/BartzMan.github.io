Plates II–VI · Services

Five kinds oftree work.

Each plate is one job, photographed on their own jobs. Under every plate: what it is, and the habitat, meaning when you would need it.

Plate II

Climbing & trimming

A climber goes up on ropes to cut back limbs that are too high, or too tangled, to reach from the ground.

Habitat: big yard trees with limbs over the roof, the drive or the fence.

Ask about trimming

Plate III

Tree removal

Taking a tree down in pieces and cutting the trunk into sections small enough to load and move.

Habitat: dead, hollow or storm-split trees, or one that has outgrown the yard.

Ask about a removal

Plate IV

Stump grinding

A stump grinder chews the stump down below the grass line, leaving wood chips instead of a stump.

Habitat: stumps left in lawns and beds after a tree has come down.

Ask about a stump

Plate V

Log loading

A grapple truck lifts heavy trunk sections onto the truck, so big wood does not have to be rolled or carried.

Habitat: removals with logs too heavy for hands and wheelbarrows.

Ask about log loading

Plate VI

Site clean-up

Moving brush, chips and wood off the lawn after the cutting, with a compact track loader on bigger jobs.

Habitat: any yard after a removal, when you want the grass back.

Ask about clean-up

Plate VIII · Field marks

A tree in trouble,marked up.

Birders learn field marks; so can a yard. These six signs can all be spotted from the ground. Tap a number on the plate.

Mark 1

Dead wood

Bare, leafless limbs in an otherwise leafy crown are dead. They are brittle and are often the first to drop in wind.

Mark 2

A crack at the fork

A split where two big limbs or trunks meet can open wider under the weight of wet leaves or a storm.

Mark 3

Hollows and soft wood

Cavities, crumbly wood or old wounds on the trunk can mean there is decay inside.

Mark 4

Mushrooms at the base

Fungus growing on the roots or the base of the trunk is often a sign of rot below the ground.

Mark 5

Lifted soil

New cracks in the soil, or a raised mound on one side of the trunk, can mean the roots are shifting.

Mark 6

Limbs over the roof

Branches resting on or hanging over the roof scrape shingles and fill gutters with leaves.

Look from the ground only. Anything touching a power line is a job for the utility company.
