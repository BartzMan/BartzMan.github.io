What a ranch gate needs

Every gate is welded to order — the notes below list what goes into each build.

  • Frame

    Square-tube steel frame with a diagonal brace — the shape that keeps a wide gate square as it swings.

  • Posts

    Heavy steel posts set deep in concrete footings, sized for the span and the wind.

  • Hinges

    Adjustable barrel hinges with grease points — heavy enough to carry the leaf without sag.

  • Latch

    A two-way latch that works from the saddle side or from the porch.

  • Finish

    Primed and painted in the color you pick, or bare steel left to weather dark.

Sizes, hardware and color get decided on the phone or at the gate — no two spans are the same.

Useful

Texas weather, steel and cedar.

Steel
The sun

Paint fades before steel fails — chalking and dull patches are the early signs. A wash and a fresh coat go a long way.

Rain & rust

Rust starts where water sits: hinge pins, latch pockets, the ground line. Keep those spots painted and drained.

Hinges & latches

Moving parts wear first. Oil the pins, and check that the gate still closes square — a dragging leaf is the first sign.

Cedar
The sun

Texas sun bleaches cedar to a silver-grey. It still holds, but a coat of UV oil now and then keeps the warm color.

Water & rot

Rot starts where wood meets the ground and where sprinklers hit. Keep the bottom rail dry and the pickets clear of soil.

Hardware & fasteners

Coated screws hold cedar best. Walk the line each season and swap any fastener that bleeds rust into the wood.
