Night — the lamp is on

Four questions
from the panel.

Q1The breaker keeps tripping.

A breaker that trips is doing its job, and the question is why. An overloaded circuit, a motor that has worn out, or a short somewhere in the wall all look identical from the panel. Tell us what was plugged in or running when it went, and that is usually most of the conversation.

Q2Can you add recessed lights to a room that already has some?

Yes. Whether that means adding to a run that is already there, opening a new one, or moving the ones already in the ceiling depends on what is above the drywall and what the circuit is already carrying. A photo of the room and a photo of the attic side answer most of it before anybody climbs anything.

Q3What do outdoor timers and circuits actually do?

An outdoor timer switches something on a schedule: landscape lighting, a fountain, a driveway light. It wants its own weatherproof circuit rather than an extension cord run across a lawn. That is the whole job: the box, the conduit, and a circuit that only does that one thing.

Q4What should I send with the message?

A photo of the panel with the door open, a photo of whatever is not working, and the address. If a breaker is tripping, add what was running when it went. If it is a lighting question, a photo of the ceiling and the switch is more useful than a description of either.
