Follow the runoff.

Rain tests seams, roof edges and wall junctions. A repair may address an open transition or an outlet that no longer drains.

Watch the thaw.

Melting snow can reveal trouble where roof edges and drainage paths meet. A closer look may identify the path water is taking.

Look along the edges.

Wind can lift edges or loosen exposed pieces. A repair can secure or replace the affected material once the surrounding roof is assessed.

Look for surface wear.

Prolonged exposure can leave some roof materials worn or brittle. An assessment can help decide whether a small repair is enough.

Pick the
roof shape.

Flat roofing

A low-slope roof uses a continuous covering and drainage paths to carry water away from the surface.

Shingle roofing

Shingles overlap to shed rain down the slope. Repairs address loose, missing or damaged pieces and the details around them.

At the wall

Roof to siding.

Siding is the outer wall covering. Damaged or loose pieces can leave the wall beneath exposed to the weather.

Ask about siding

Look from
the ground.

Once conditions have settled, make a short record of what changed outside and inside.

Look along the roof edge

Note missing shingles, hanging trim or changes at visible gutters and downspouts.

Scan siding and nearby ground

Look for loose siding or pieces that have fallen, and take photos of anything you can see clearly.

Check indoors for new marks

Record new stains near ceilings and windows, including where they appeared and when you first noticed them.
