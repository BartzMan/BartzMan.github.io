THE PORCH, MASKED AND READY — WHAT A FRESH COAT LOOKS LIKE MID-JOB

press with your palm, not your nail!

Rub a flat palm on the wall and look at your hand. White dust means the paint has chalked — the sun has eaten the binder and the next coat will not stick until the chalk is washed off.

Peeling usually starts where water sits: under the eave, at the base, around a window. Lift the loose flap and look at what is under it — paint peeling off paint means the old coat is already letting go.

Thin cracks that follow the stucco's own movement are normal desert behaviour. What matters is whether the crack is open to the weather — a coat that spans it keeps the monsoon rain out of the wall behind.

Stand in the shade and compare the south wall to the north. If the south has washed out a tone lighter, the sun has done its work and the wall is ready for the next coat — at the colour you actually want.
