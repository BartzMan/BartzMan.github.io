Plate III Classification

Four families of work.

I

Roof replacement

A full new roof: the old covering comes off and new shingles go on. Worth asking about when a roof is worn out or storm damage is widespread.

II

Roof repair

A targeted fix to one problem area, like a leak, a few lifted or missing shingles or a damaged vent collar. Right when the rest of the roof is sound.

III

Hail & storm damage inspections

A close look at the roof after hail or wind, so you know what the storm did before you decide anything. Worth doing even when nothing shows from the ground.

IV

Metal accent roofs

Standing-seam metal over a bay window, porch or gable adds a crisp line of colour to a shingle roof. The teal on this brick home is one example.

Plate IV Field notes

Read your roof from the street.

Five parts to look at after DFW hail. Binoculars from the ground are enough. Never climb up there: if something looks off, photograph it and call a roofer.

0 of 5 logged

  1. 1 Ridge caps

    Is the peak line straight and unbroken?

  2. 2 Valleys

    Any ragged shingles or debris piled where two slopes meet?

  3. 3 Vents and boots

    Dented caps, or cracked collars around the pipes?

  4. 4 Gutters and downspouts

    Dents, or loose grit from shingles collecting at the outlet?

  5. 5 Drip edge

    Bent or dented metal along the eaves?
