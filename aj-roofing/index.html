02Roof & wall, in section

What stands between the weather and your house.

A roof and a wall are one system. Scroll and the section builds the way it goes on the house: deck first, siding last.

  1. Roof deckThe plywood or OSB sheets the whole roof is nailed to. When old shingles come off, soft spots show up here first.
  2. Underlayment & ice barrierA water-shedding sheet over the deck, with a sticky membrane along the eaves where Ohio snow melts, refreezes and backs up.
  3. Drip edgeThe metal edge that throws water off the roof and into the gutter instead of behind the fascia.
  4. ShinglesArchitectural shingles, laid in overlapping courses from the eave up to the ridge.
  5. Ridge ventLets warm, wet attic air out along the peak, so the deck stays dry from underneath.
  6. Fascia & soffitThe trim boards at the eave, where the roof hands water and air over to the wall.
  7. Sheathing & housewrapThe wall's own deck, wrapped against wind and water before any siding goes on.
  8. SidingLap boards, each one overlapping the one below, the same idea as the shingles above.

Rarely in the middle of a roof. They start where a slope runs into a siding wall, around a skylight, along a tired fascia board. That line is exactly where a roofer and a siding contractor tend to point at each other.
