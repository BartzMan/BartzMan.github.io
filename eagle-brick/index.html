03 · How it's laid

What sits under a paver surface.

A paver surface is only as flat as the ground under it. The part you see is the top two inches; the rest is sand, base and an edge that keeps the field from creeping outwards. Here is the usual order of a paver job, in plain words.

  1. 01

    Look at the ground

    Where water runs, what is there now, what has to come out.

  2. 02

    Dig out & base

    Old surface removed, base spread and compacted in layers.

  3. 03

    Sand bed

    A thin layer of bedding sand, screeded level for the pavers.

  4. 04

    Lay the pattern

    Herringbone, running bond or a border: cut in at the edges.

  5. 05

    Edge & joints

    Edge restraint set, joint sand swept in, surface compacted.

  6. 06

    Seal, if wanted

    A sealer coat once the pavers are clean and dry.

General outline only. Every job starts by looking at the ground it will sit on.

04 · Seal it

Why pavers get sealed.

Concrete pavers are porous. Under the Gulf coast sun, sprinkler water and salt air, colour can fade and joint sand can wash out. A sealer is a coat over the top that closes the surface up.

Water beads off

Spills and rain sit on top for longer instead of soaking straight in.

Colour holds

Many sealers deepen the colour and slow the bleaching from the sun.

Joints stay put

Sealed joint sand is harder for rain and hoses to wash away.

General information about paver sealers. Ask Eagle Brick what suits your pavers when you call.
