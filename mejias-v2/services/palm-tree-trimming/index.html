Palms look effortless until the fronds start to brown and the trunk goes shaggy. Dead fronds can drop without warning, hanging seed and fruit clusters make a mess on patios and pools, and untrimmed palms near a house, fence or pool are more than a cosmetic problem. Regular trimming keeps them neat and takes the hazards out of the picture.

Every palm is different, and how much to take off depends on the variety, its health and the season. We talk it through before we start, and we're happy to explain what we're removing and why.

Good to know

Green fronds do real work

Palms make their food in their green fronds, so the usual guidance is to take dead, brown and hazardous fronds and be careful with healthy green ones. How much comes off depends on the variety, the palm's health and the season. We'll talk it through.

Common questions

When is the best time to trim palms in Mesa?
Many Arizona tree pros recommend late spring to early summer (roughly May to June), ahead of monsoon season, so loose fronds don't become flying debris in storm winds. Some homeowners add a second cleanup in late fall. Ask us about your palms.
How often do palms need trimming?
Once or twice a year is typical for most landscape palms, depending on the variety and how tidy you like them.
Can you trim very tall palms?
Send us a photo and the approximate height and we'll tell you how we'd approach it. Tall palms are a safety job, and we'd rather look first than guess.
Do you haul away the fronds and seed pods?
Yes. Cleaning up is part of the job, so you're not left with piles of fronds in the yard.
