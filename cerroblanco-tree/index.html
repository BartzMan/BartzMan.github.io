No room for a crane?We climb.

Plenty of Central Florida trees grow where a crane can't reach: behind the fence, over the pool cage, tight against the roofline. That is where a climber goes up instead, ties in, and the tree comes down in pieces small enough to lower and carry out.

Where the bucket truck fits, the basket goes up into the oak. Either way the cutting happens up top, one piece at a time.

Logs out.Brush chipped.

Every job ends on the ground. The grapple loader carries the logs, limbs go through the chipper at the curb, and the saw cuts the trunk into pieces that can be moved. Debris and log removal is part of the job, not a separate visit.

And the stump?

Stump grinding takes what is left of the trunk down into chips so the spot can be leveled and planted or turfed. Ask for it with the removal, or call about an old stump on its own.

Can you trim a live oak and keep its shape?

Yes. Trimming a live oak is mostly lifting limbs off the roof and the driveway, taking out dead wood and branches that rub, and thinning where it gets heavy, so the oak still looks like an oak. Send a photo and say what is bothering you about it.

The tree is right against the house. Can it still come down?

That is the kind of tree a climber is for. Instead of dropping it whole, it comes down in sections, lowered on rope where there is no room to let pieces fall. We look at it first and then tell you how it would come down.

Do you grind the stump too?

Yes. Stump grinding can go with a removal or be booked on its own for an old stump. Tell us roughly how wide it is and whether a gate or fence is in the way.

What should I send before you come out?

A photo of the whole tree from far enough back to see the top, one of the base, and the street or ZIP. Text it to (321) 987-2999 or email it to cerroblancotreeandlawn@gmail.com.
