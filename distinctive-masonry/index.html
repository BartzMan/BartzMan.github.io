Course 03 · Running bond · Stoop check

Climb the stoop.Check every step.

Freeze and thaw is hard on New York masonry. Each step of this stoop is one check you can do yourself in spring, from the sidewalk up to the door. Tap a step.

Step 1 of 6

Mortar joints

Scratch a joint with a key. If the mortar crumbles to sand, the joints need repointing before more water gets in.

Step 2 of 6

Flaking brick

Brick faces flaking or popping off, called spalling, mean water got into the brick and froze.

Step 3 of 6

Cracked steps

Look for cracks across the treads, and for stair-step cracks running along the joints of the side walls.

Step 4 of 6

White powder

A white, chalky bloom on brick means water is moving through the masonry and leaving salts behind.

Step 5 of 6

Loose rails & caps

Gently test the railing and the caps on the piers. Anything that moves needs resetting before someone leans on it.

Step 6 of 6

Puddles

Water that stands on the stoop or the walk, or pavers that have sunk, point to a drainage problem.
