Winter prep

Winter and storm questions.

Freeze-thaw. Water gets into small cracks, freezes and widens them, then thaws and does it again. That is why winter is hard on roofs, brick and siding. General information only.

Q1Metal roof or shingles: what is the difference?

Shingles are layered asphalt pieces. Metal roofing is long panels fastened over the roof deck. Metal panels are smooth, so snow tends to slide off in sheets, while shingles hold it a little longer. Which suits a house depends on the roof, the budget and the look you want, so it is worth talking through.

Q2What should I check on the roof after a winter storm?

From the ground, look for missing or lifted shingles or panels, bent metal at the edges, long icicles along the eaves and new stains on ceilings. Stay off the roof yourself: photos taken from the ground are enough to start. Text them to (740) 951-0040.

Q3Do roof, window and siding jobs need a permit?

It depends on the city, village or township and on the kind of job. It is worth asking your local building department early, and permit questions are worked out as part of planning the work.

Q4What is an ice dam?

An ice dam is a ridge of ice that builds up along the edge of a roof when melting snow refreezes there. Long icicles and a thick band of ice along the eaves are the usual signs. It is general information only, so call or text if you are worried about a roof.

Q5What should I send when I text?

Photos of the roof, windows, siding or brick you want looked at, your town or ZIP code, and what you have noticed. Text them to (740) 951-0040.
