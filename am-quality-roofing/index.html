7:15 AM The view from the ridge

Know your roof, part by part.

Tap a marker on the drone view to read what that part does and what to look for.

Ridge cap

The peak where two roof planes meet, covered with shaped shingles. It sheds water down both sides and shuts out wind-driven rain.

Look for: cap pieces that are lifted, cracked or missing along the line.

Roof vents

These boxes let hot, damp air out of the attic so the space under your roof can breathe. Air moving through the attic helps shingles and wood last.

Look for: a vent that is cracked, crooked or sitting open to the rain.

Pipe flashing

The collar sealed around a plumbing vent pipe so rain cannot run down it into the house. The rubber seal is a common place for small leaks to start.

Look for: a split or dried-out collar, or a stain on the ceiling below.

Chimney and flashing

Metal flashing steps up the brick where a chimney meets the roof. That joint moves with weather, so it is a place to watch after storms.

Look for: gaps along the metal, loose mortar or a stain on the wall near the chimney.

The shingle field

Overlapping courses shed water downhill, one row over the next. Most of the roof's job is done here.

Look for: from the ground, pieces that are lifted, curled, cracked or missing. Never climb up to check.

9:20 AM Before the work starts

Solar panels and a new roof: the order things happen in.

Panels cannot stay on while the roof under them is replaced. This is the usual order, so you know what to expect and what to ask.

1. Panels are powered down

Before anyone touches the array, it is switched off safely.

2. Panels come off and are stored

Panels are lifted off and set aside somewhere safe while the roof is open.

3. Old roof off, deck checked

With the old shingles gone, the wood deck underneath can be checked and fixed.

4. The new roof goes on

Underlayment and shingles go down, with the panel mounting spots sealed against water.

5. Panels go back on and are reconnected

The array returns to the roof and is reconnected and tested before it is switched on.

Three things to ask anyone quoting the job

  1. Who powers down and removes the panels?
  2. Who reconnects and tests them afterwards?
  3. Who do I call if something looks wrong?
