ROAD TEST · FROM THE GROUND ONLY · NEVER CLIMB

After a Panhandle hailstorm,
four checks from the ground.

Hail talks loud and tells the truth later. Walk the yard, look at these four things, and you will know whether the roof needs a closer look — no ladder, no climbing. Tap a marker to open it.

THE SHINGLES, LOOKED AT FROM THE YARD — WHERE EVERY CHECK STARTS

check the low places first!

Grab a handful of what washed down. Black grit and granules in the gutter mean the shingle surface is losing its coat, even if the roof still looks fine from the street.

Walk the fence line and look at the soft metal — vents, flashing, the back of the mailbox. Dents there tell you how hard the stones were hitting the roof itself, from right where you stand.

Scan the slope from the yard for stripes, bare patches and pieces lying in the grass. A missing shingle is a door left open for the next rain — note where it is and what is under it.

A ceiling ring that grows after every rain tracks a leak to the field above it. Write down which room, photograph the stain, and that note is worth bringing along to anyone who looks at the roof.
