Heat pump or furnace?

Winters around San Jose are mild, so heating often does light work. That is why heat pumps come up so much here: they move heat rather than burn fuel, and the same outdoor unit cools in summer. Gas furnaces still make sense in some homes. Compare the two, then take the questions to whoever quotes your job.

Heat pump

How it works
It moves heat from outside to inside to warm the house, and reverses to cool it in summer.
Why it suits mild winters
When winters stay mild, a heat pump is rarely pushed hard, and one system covers both heating and cooling.
Might not be the best fit when
The electrical panel is already full, or the house has heavy heating needs and ducts that cannot be changed.
Ask about
A proper load calculation, the condition of the ducts and whether the panel can take it.

Gas furnace

How it works
It burns gas to heat air, which the air handler moves through the ducts. Cooling comes from a separate air conditioner.
Why people keep them
A gas line and ducts are often already there, so a furnace can be the simplest swap in a home that works well as it is.
Might not be the best fit when
You want one system for heating and cooling, or the flue and gas line are awkward to run.
Ask about
Venting and gas line condition, and how a furnace compares with a heat pump for your house.
