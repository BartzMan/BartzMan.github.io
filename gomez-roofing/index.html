Course 1 What we do

Four jobs, one straight line.

01

Roof replacement

A full new roof: the old shingles come off, the deck is checked, and new underlayment and shingles go on in straight courses. You need it when wear is widespread, not just in one spot.

02

Roof repair

Fixing the part that is failing without replacing the rest: lifted or missing shingles, a small leak, a worn patch. It makes sense when the rest of the roof is still in good shape.

03

Vents & flashing

Every pipe, vent and chimney is a hole in the roof, and the metal around it keeps water out. You need this when a leak shows up near one of them, or the flashing looks worn.

04

Storm damage repairs

Houston weather is hard on roofs. After a storm, damaged or missing shingles and loosened flashing get fixed so the next rain stays outside.

Course 2 Roof penetration clinic

Every hole in the roof is a place to check.

Pipes, vents, chimneys and valleys are where leaks usually start. Tap a numbered spot on the roof to see what usually fails first, how it shows up inside, and what the fix looks like.

Drawn roof, not to scale. Tap a number.

Spot 1

Pipe boot

The rubber collar around a plumbing vent pipe.

Fails first
The rubber collar cracks and shrinks in the sun, so water runs down the pipe.
Shows up inside
A ring-shaped stain on the ceiling near a pipe, or a damp streak down the wall beside it.
The fix
Swap the boot or collar and reseal the base under the shingles above it.

Spot 2

Box vent

The low square vent that lets attic air out.

Fails first
Sealant around the base dries out, nails back out, or the plastic hood cracks.
Shows up inside
A stain on the ceiling under the vent, or damp insulation in the attic below it.
The fix
Reset or replace the vent and flash it shingle-style so the upper shingles shed water over it.

Spot 3

Turbine

The spinning vent that pulls hot air out of the attic.

Fails first
The bearing wears so it wobbles or squeals, and the base flashing lifts.
Shows up inside
Noise in wind, or a drip at the ceiling below it during wind-driven rain.
The fix
Replace the turbine, or swap it for a low-profile vent, and re-flash the opening.

Spot 4

Chimney flashing

The metal that steps up the side of a brick chimney.

Fails first
The sealant line at the brick cracks, and the metal pulls away from the mortar.
Shows up inside
A stain on the wall or ceiling beside the chimney, usually after heavy rain.
The fix
New step and counter flashing worked into the mortar joint and woven in with the shingles.

Spot 5

Valley

The V where two roof slopes meet and share their water.

Fails first
Worn shingle edges, a stray nail, or leaves packed in the channel.
Shows up inside
A long stain on the ceiling that follows the line where the roof planes meet.
The fix
Clear it, replace worn sections, and reline the valley with new metal or shingles.

Spot 6

Skylight curb

The raised frame a skylight sits on, and the metal around it.

Fails first
The corner seals and flashing pieces open up as the roof moves with heat and cold.
Shows up inside
Drips or staining at the edge of the skylight or down the light shaft.
The fix
New flashing and sealant around the curb; an old unit may need replacing too.

Course 3 Which job is it?

Patch it or start over?

A repair usually fits when…

  • a few shingles are lifted, cracked or missing
  • one leak traces back to a single vent, pipe or flashing
  • storm marks sit in one area of the roof
  • the rest of the roof still lies flat and looks even

Fixing the failing part keeps the good roof you already have working.

A replacement usually fits when…

  • shingles are curling, cracking or bald across whole slopes
  • leaks keep appearing in different places
  • repairs have been needed again and again
  • the deck feels soft or the roof sags in places

A full new roof starts from the deck up, on a fresh chalk line.

Course 4 Before you call

Small roof leak? 5 places it usually starts.

Water travels, so the stain is rarely right under the leak. Check from the ground, or from the attic with a torch, and tick each spot as you go. Never climb onto the roof to look. If something looks wrong, call a roofer.

0 of 5 checked

  1. Look for cracked or split rubber around plumbing pipes, or a stain on the ceiling near one.

  2. Check attic vents from below for daylight, rust or damp insulation, and from the ground for cracked hoods.

  3. Look at the wall and ceiling beside the chimney for stains, and from the ground for gaps in the metal.

  4. Follow the line where two roof slopes meet. A long stain on the ceiling under it is a clue.

  5. Small dark specks or bumps on the roof from the ground, or tiny drip marks in the attic under the deck.
