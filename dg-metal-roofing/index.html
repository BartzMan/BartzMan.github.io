Bold and warm. A deep red shows off the shape of a roof, and suits tile-style profiles. Dark colours take on more heat than light ones.

Residential metal roofing

New metal roofs for houses: porches, additions and whole-house roofs, in standing seam and other profiles.

  • Pick a colour that suits the house and the street.
  • Roof shape and pitch help decide the profile.
  • Trim and flashing finish every edge.

Commercial metal roofing

Metal roofs for shops, offices, workshops and other larger buildings, where long, clean panel runs suit a wide, low roof.

  • Long panels cover wide spans in fewer pieces.
  • A low pitch calls for the right profile and seams.
  • Colour can match a brand or the surroundings.

Metal siding & trim

Metal wall panels and trim that match the roof, for a finished exterior from the eave down.

  • Panels and trim can share one colour family.
  • Corners, windows and edges get their own trim pieces.
  • A good pairing with a Galvalume or white roof.

Profile picker

Three ways a metal roof can look.

  • Smooth flat panels with raised seams.
  • Fasteners sit under the seam, out of sight.
  • The profile for a clean, modern line.
  • Ribbed panels, screwed down through the flat.
  • Screw heads show; they are checked and replaced over time.
  • Common on barns, sheds, carports and porches.
  • Steel panels pressed with a rounded tile shape.
  • Often chosen for Mediterranean and Spanish-style homes.
  • Lighter to carry than real clay tile.
Before you sign

Metal roof near the Gulf: 5 questions to ask.

  1. Near the coast, salt speeds up wear. Ask which paint system and coating the panels carry and how it suits salt-air spots.

  2. Ask what the fasteners are made of, and whether they sit under the seam or show on the panel face.

  3. Ask which underlayment goes over the deck, and how the deck gets checked before the metal goes on.

  4. Ask about the pieces at valleys, walls, chimneys and edges. Most leaks start where the panels stop.

  5. Ask how edge trim and gutters are fastened to stand up to strong coastal wind.
