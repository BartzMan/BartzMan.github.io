Know the
surface.

Choose the material closest to the roof you see from the ground.

Shingles

Layered asphalt shingles shed water down the slope. Missing tabs or lifted edges are useful details to point out.

Tile

Overlapping tiles shed water onto the courses below. Cracked pieces and leaks at junctions can be clues to check the layer underneath.

Standing seam

Raised seams join metal panels along the slope. Notice any changes around the edges and where vents meet the panels.

Bay Area roof guide

Moss or algae?

Both can show up where the roof stays shaded or damp. Start by observing from the ground.

Look at the pattern

Moss tends to form raised clumps. Algae often looks like dark streaks or a thin stain.

Note where it returns

Take a photo of the affected slope, then note overhanging branches, shade and nearby gutters.

Skip forceful cleaning

Avoid scraping or pressure-washing shingles. Ask a roofer which treatment suits the material and how the roof drains.

Care follows
the weather.

Choose a point on the Bay roof-care calendar.

Dry season

Look over the roof from the ground while it is dry. Note any loose edges or damaged areas that stand out.

Before rain

Look for leaves and overflow marks around gutters and downspouts. Note any areas that backed up in the last rain.

After a storm

When conditions are calm, take a ground-level look for changes and check indoors for new stains.
