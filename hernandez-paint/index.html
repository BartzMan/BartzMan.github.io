Start to finish

How a room gets painted.

The usual order for an interior repaint, from a bare room to the last pass. Every house is different, so ask about yours. Tap a step.

  1. Furniture to the middle of the room, floors and fixtures under drop cloths, switch plates and outlet covers off.

  2. Nail holes and dents filled, sanded flat and wiped down, so the new coat grips and nothing shows through.

  3. Bare patches, stains and big color changes get a primer first, so the finish coat dries to one even color.

  4. A brush lines the corners, ceiling and trim; the roller fills the wall in long, overlapping passes while the edges are still wet.

  5. A second coat once the first is dry, then a slow walk round the room in good light to catch any thin spot.

Edge to edge.

One wet edge, one direction, no lap marks. A wall looks finished when you cannot tell where one pass ended and the next began.

Useful before you call

Which sheen for which room?

Sheen is how much dried paint shines. More shine wipes clean easily but shows every bump; less shine hides flaws but marks sooner. Pick a room.

Less shineMore shine

  • Flat
  • Eggshell
  • Satin
  • Semi-gloss
  • Gloss

Ceilings: flat. It hides small bumps and won't bounce lamp light back at you. Nobody touches a ceiling.

Bedrooms and living rooms: eggshell. A soft glow that wipes clean better than flat, on walls people lean on.

Kitchens and baths: satin or semi-gloss. Steam and splashes need a finish you can wipe. Go shinier where it gets wettest.

Trim, doors and cabinets: semi-gloss or gloss. Hands touch them daily; a harder shine cleans up and keeps edges crisp.

Outside walls: flat or satin. Flat hides dents in old siding; satin sheds dust and rain a little better.
