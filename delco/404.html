Igniters, flame sensors, inducers, limit switches, and the question nobody wants: is the heat exchanger intact? We test before we guess. A replacement is sized to the house, not to whatever cabinet is on the truck.

Oil burners and older heat

Oil is still in plenty of South Shore basements. We service burners and talk straight about what a changeover actually involves. If the utility or a fuel company has to be in the middle of it, we say that instead of improvising.

Seasonal maintenance

A fall combustion and safety check on the heat. A spring cleaning and electrical check on the air conditioner. Most January emergencies started as a small October problem.

Thermostats and zoning

A thermostat in the wrong place, or zones that were never set up, will make a good system feel broken. Upstairs roasting while the living room is cold is often a control problem, not a reason to buy a bigger unit.

Before you call

Six checks that save a wasted trip.

If one of these fixes it, good — you didn’t need us yet. If it doesn’t, you’ll sound like someone who already looked, and the visit starts further along.

  1. 01

    Thermostat

    Set to heat or cool, and set past the room temperature. If it takes batteries, swap them before you assume the equipment is dead.

  2. 02

    Breaker

    One reset on the air handler, furnace, or boiler breaker is fair. If it trips again, stop. Repeated trips mean something is wrong — call.

  3. 03

    Filter

    On forced air, a filter that looks like a gray brick will mimic a dead system. If you can see through it, leave it. If you can't, change it and give the system twenty minutes.

  4. 04

    Boiler pressure

    Glance at the gauge. If it's sitting at zero, don't keep adding water to 'get through the night.' Water you add is water that left. That leak is the job.

  5. 05

    Outdoor unit

    The disconnect switch beside the condenser should be on, and the coil shouldn't be buried in shrubs. Cottonwood and a crushed disconnect fool a lot of no-cool calls.

  6. 06

    If you smell gas

    Leave the house. Call your gas utility and 911. Don't hunt for the leak, don't flip switches, and don't wait on a contractor.

FAQ

Questions worth answering before the truck rolls.

Do you work on Staten Island boilers?+

Yes. Hot-water and steam boilers are a core part of the work — no-heat calls, circulators, zone valves, aquastats, pressure problems, and full replacements when the boiler itself is finished. A lot of 10312 housing stock was built around a boiler, not a furnace in a closet.

Which neighborhoods do you cover?+

The shop is at 56 Bamberger Lane, so Annadale, Eltingville, Great Kills, Huguenot, Arden Heights, Prince's Bay, Pleasant Plains, Rossville, Woodrow, Tottenville, and Charleston are closest. We serve the rest of Staten Island too. If the house is on the island, call.

How do I get on the schedule?+

Call (347) 286-6798 or send a note through this page. Say what's happening, what kind of equipment you have if you know it, and your neighborhood. We'll tell you the earliest opening and whether the visit is a diagnostic or a replacement look — including what that visit costs — before anyone drives over.

Will you tell me to replace a system I could repair?+

Not if the repair is the better spend. Equipment under about eight years old is almost always a repair unless there's a safety issue. Older equipment with a failed heat exchanger, a cracked section, or a compressor that costs more than the system is worth is a different conversation — and you'll hear both numbers before you decide.

Do you install ductless mini-splits?+

Yes. They're the right tool for a finished basement, an addition, a closed-in porch, or a bedroom the existing ducts never really served. One head, heating and cooling, without opening the walls of the rest of the house.

Can you help with oil heat?+

We service oil burners and older heating plants still common on the South Shore. If you're thinking about leaving oil, we'll explain what the mechanical side involves and what has to go through your fuel company or the gas utility. We won't pretend one trade covers every permit on a conversion.

What are your hours?+

The scheduling line is Monday through Friday, 9am to 5pm. Messages after that are returned the next business morning. If the house is cold or the air is down during the day, call — don't wait on a form.

Can I stop by the shop?+

Please call first. 56 Bamberger Lane is our base on Staten Island, not a showroom with a counter. The work happens at your house.
