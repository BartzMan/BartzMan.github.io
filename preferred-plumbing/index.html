CClay · the Ohio year

Freeze, thaw, storm, shut-down.

Winter: when water turns to ice.

Water expands as it freezes and squeezes a pipe from the inside. Pipes in unheated spaces and along cold outside walls are the usual places for trouble.

  • A tap that runs slow or not at all on a very cold morning
  • Frost on a pipe in the garage or crawl space
  • A hose left on an outdoor spigot

If a pipe has split: turn the water off at the main shutoff and call a plumber.

Service that covers itLeak and pipe repair

Spring: thaw, rain and a busy sump.

As the ground thaws and the rain comes, water collects around the foundation. A sump pit and pump move it outside, and spring is when they work hardest.

  • A pump that never runs, or runs all the time
  • Damp patches on the basement floor or walls
  • A discharge pipe that has come loose outside

Quick test: pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start.

Summer: storms and heavy rain.

Downpours can overload drains, and a power cut can stop a sump pump just when it is needed. Slow drains in the lowest part of the house are an early sign of a blocked line.

  • Gurgling from drains during heavy rain
  • Water pooling around a floor drain
  • Slow sinks and tubs on the lowest level

Tip: keep floor drains clear of laundry lint and debris.

Service that covers itDrain and fixture work

Fall: the shut-down checklist.

Autumn is the calm before the freeze. Take hoses off, drain the outdoor taps, look over the water heater and make sure the main shutoff still turns.

  • Hoses still attached to outdoor taps
  • A water heater that is noisy, leaking or giving rusty water
  • A main shutoff that is stiff or will not turn
