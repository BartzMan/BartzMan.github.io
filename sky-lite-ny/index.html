Light-level guide

Read the room.

Lux measures how much light reaches a surface. Pick a space to see an example range.

Typical planning range / Warehouse

100–200 lx

High-bay fixtures can distribute light over storage aisles. Keep separate task needs in mind.

Typical planning range / Office

200–500 lx

Linear fixtures or flat panels can provide general light, with a desk light where detail work needs it.

Typical planning range / Retail floor

300–500 lx

Use general lighting for aisles and consider track heads where a display needs a separate accent.

Typical planning range / Parking lot

20–50 lx

Pole-mounted area lights distribute light across open space. Placement and glare matter near edges and entrances.

Typical planning range / Home kitchen

320–430 lx

Ceiling lights cover the room. Under-cabinet fixtures can bring light onto the counter.

Typical planning examples. Actual targets depend on the tasks, layout and daylight.

Planning an LED switch

Make a light list.

Bring a room-by-room list when you ask for a quote. It gives the conversation a clear starting point.

01Rooms and uses

Write down the rooms and what people do in each, including any work surfaces that need a closer look.

02Existing fixtures

Count fixtures by room. Take a photo of each style from the ground and note any that flicker.

03Controls and hours

List switches, dimmers and sensors. Note when each area normally needs light.

04Glare and shadows

Mark where screens reflect light, shelves cast shadows, or work surfaces look dim.
