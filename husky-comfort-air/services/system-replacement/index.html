The job

What replacement involves.

A replacement is different from a fresh install because something is already there. The old condenser, coil and often the air handler come out, the refrigerant is recovered properly, and the new equipment goes in using whatever existing ductwork, wiring and platforms are still in good shape.

Outdoor and indoor sections are designed as a pair. Putting a new condenser on an old indoor coil can leave the system short of what it was built to do, so replacing both together is usually the cleaner long-term answer. The ducts get a look at the same time, since new equipment pushing air through leaky ducts wastes part of the upgrade.

Check list

Signs it may be time

  • Repeat repairsSomething new fails every season
  • Compressor failureOn an older system
  • Older refrigerantA type that is being phased out
  • Runs nonstopRooms still warm by evening
  • Comfort droppingBills climbing at the same time
  • Visible wearRust, oil stains or a leaking coil

The visit

How a replacement goes.

  1. Step 01

    Repair or replace?

    Before talking about new equipment, the current system is checked so you know whether a repair is still a sensible option for it.

  2. Step 02

    Measure and match

    The house, the ducts and the existing equipment are looked at together to choose a capacity and an indoor and outdoor pair that suit each other.

  3. Step 03

    Old out, new in

    Refrigerant is recovered, the old units are disconnected and removed, and the new equipment is set, connected, evacuated and charged.

  4. Step 04

    Test the whole system

    Airflow, temperatures and drain flow are checked at start-up, and you are walked through the new thermostat and filter.

System replacement

What happens next

Big units, lifted into place.

Not every replacement sits on the ground. Husky Comfort Air's own photos include a rooftop unit going up on a crane, which is how a heavy package unit reaches a flat commercial roof.

Whether the equipment is on a pad, in the attic or on the roof, the job ends the same way: the system running and checked, with a clear picture of what was replaced and why.
