The hail size guide — read a storm from the ground

Pick a roof. Pick a stone.

In the Basin the question after every storm is the same one: how big was it? Switch the material, switch the stone, and the diagram marks what each size does to that roof — before anyone climbs up.

The roof is

The hail came down as

Asphalt shingle roof — pea-size hail diagram shown

Shingle: granules only

Pea-size hail pocks the granule surface — fresh dark speckles on the tabs, small pits you can feel with a palm. A quarter-inch stone rarely breaks through the mat. Log it, date it, and let the next storm tell you more.

Metal: it rings and shrugs

Pea-size stones dent nothing on exposed-fastener steel — you mostly hear it. Check the sealant collars at the screws anyway: fresh chips around a fastener mean the stone hit hard enough to matter.

Flat: a timestamp

Pea hail leaves speckles in the membrane surfacing and little else. Its real value is the record: if the roof already carried older marks, a new layer of them tells you how the storms are stacking up.

Shingle: bruised tabs

Quarter-size hail leaves bruises — crushed granules, soft spots under the palm, edges still lying flat. Crews count bruises per square, and the count decides repair versus replacement.

Metal: first real dents

Quarter-size hail starts dimpling the panels between the ribs. Cosmetic on most gauges, but every dent is a record of angle and force — and dents collect under the eaves first.

Flat: scuffs at the seams

Quarter hail scuffs the membrane and can lift the edge of an aged seam. A scuff sheds water fine; a lifted seam is where the next rain finds its way in.

Shingle: the replacement line

Golf-ball hail breaks mats. Tabs crack, corners lift, and the surface stamps in like a hammer blow. Most shingle roofs that take a golf storm end up as full replacements.

Metal: panels need a look

Golf-ball hail dims panels across their flats and can knock ribs out of line at the eaves. The metal keeps shedding water; the question is the fastener lines and the ridge caps.

Flat: punctures begin

Golf hail is where flat roofs start taking real hits — punctures through the membrane, splits along old seams, cracks at curbs and scuppers. Every puncture is a leak waiting for the next rain.

Shingle: down to the deck

Baseball hail goes through. Punctures land in the decking, courses shatter, and part of the roof is an open hole with a tarp question attached. That is a full replacement, with the deck inspected board by board.

Metal: panels give up

Baseball hail tears through steel and bends ribs flat. Panels come off for a real look at what is under them, and whatever took a direct hit usually needs new metal, not patching.

Flat: the roof is a strainer

Baseball hail punctures a flat membrane in dozens of places at once, and the interior shows it fast. The rebuild is the whole membrane — seams first, drains last.

Residential & commercial

One crew, two ledgers.

A house and a storefront roof are different trades: shingle courses against membrane seams. Open the ledger that matches the building.

Shingle country

Houses here mostly wear shingle, and the storm math decides most jobs: bruise counts, deck condition, then a plan. A re-roof is a two-day operation — protect the yard, tear off, re-deck where the boards ask for it, and lay the new courses up to a clean ridge.

  • Shingle replacements, ridge to eave
  • Tear-offs to sound decking
  • Metal roofs & exposed-fastener seam repair
  • Vents, valleys & flashing details

Flat work downtown

Flat and low-slope roofs are water management: a membrane over the deck, seams welded or torched, and every drop walked to a drain or a scupper. The crew stages the roof so the business underneath stays in business.

  • Commercial flat & TPO membranes
  • Parapets, curbs & scuppers
  • Drains, splits & seam repairs
  • Storefront & shop re-roofs

The work

Five jobs, one crew.

1

Shingle re-roofs

The full replacement: tear-off to the deck, new underlayment, new courses, new ridge. The yard gets tarped first and goes back the way it came.

2

Tear-offs & decking

Some roofs hide soft boards. Strip to the plywood, replace what the storm or the sun wore out, and build the new roof on solid decking.

3

Metal & seam repair

Exposed-fastener panels, screw lines and ridge caps — re-sealed where they run, re-screwed where they back out, patched where the stones landed.

4

Flat & TPO

Low-slope roofing is plumbing with a roof on it: membrane welded or torched, seams walked and tested, drains and scuppers kept clear so water leaves the roof. Storefronts, shops and warehouses across the Basin.

5

Hail & storm response

When a front comes through, the work starts with counting — bruise by bruise, panel by panel — so the damage gets sized before the crews get scheduled. That is the whole hail guide above, turned into a job.
