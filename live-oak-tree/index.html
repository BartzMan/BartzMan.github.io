Plate 2 · The tree year

Tap a season. See the work.

A live oak is evergreen, but its year still turns. Here is what an arborist usually does in each season, with photos from their own jobs.

Storm checks, and hands off the oak saw

  • Look trees over after spring storms for cracked or hanging limbs.
  • Hold off trimming oaks now: spring is oak wilt season.
  • A good moment to plan beds and decide what goes in them.

Water, mulch and wait

  • Young trees and new plantings need deep, regular watering in the heat.
  • Mulch keeps roots cooler and the soil damper.
  • Save the big cuts for cooler months.

Planting season

  • Cooler soil and air let new roots settle in before summer.
  • Fall and winter are the usual planting window in Texas.
  • Trees, shrubs and xeriscape beds all go in now.

Trim in the dry, cold months

  • Oaks are usually trimmed now, while it is cold and dry.
  • Dead trees are easy to spot and plan for in winter.
  • Clear dead wood before spring storms arrive.

Plate 3 · Specimens

Two kinds of green work.

01

Tree trimming and arbor care

Trimming takes out dead, crowded or rubbing branches so a tree stays healthy and safe to stand under.

02

Tree removal, including dead trees

A dead tree can drop limbs without warning. Removal takes it down in a controlled way, piece by piece.

03

Tree planting

Planting puts a new tree in the ground with room to grow. Fall and winter are the usual planting months here.

04

Xeriscape beds

Xeriscape means beds built around plants and stone that need little water, such as agave, palms and gravel.

05

Lawn and landscaping

A green lawn, a stone edge and a few well-placed beds give a yard its shape.

06

Clearing and prep

Every new bed starts with clearing brush and dead growth so there is clean ground to plant into.
