What goes on the house

Roofing, from the bare deck up.

Two finished roofs, two jobs that come before them. Tap Shingle or Metal to see what each one is and where it suits.

Shingle roofs

Overlapping asphalt shingles nailed in courses across the deck, each row shedding water onto the one below.

When you need it
When the roof has reached the end of its life and you want the classic look.
Where it suits
Brick and stone homes with a pitched roof, where a familiar texture blends in.

Illustrative stock photo.

Standing-seam metal

Long metal panels joined by raised seams that run from ridge to eave, so water runs straight down between them.

When you need it
When you want a crisp, modern line and a roof with very few seams to cross.
Where it suits
Homes with a steady pitch and clean lines, from a porch roof to the whole house.

Tear-off and underlayment

The old roof comes off down to the deck, then a water-resistant layer goes on first. You need it when a roof is worn out, not just patched.

Protecting beds and patios

Tarps over shrubs, beds and patio edges catch falling material. You need it on any tear-off, because the old roof has to land somewhere.
