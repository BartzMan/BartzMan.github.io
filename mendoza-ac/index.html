The approach

Repair first,
when repair makes sense.

A rooftop package unit or a mini-split that is old and still cooling should not be replaced because a new one is easier to quote. We look at the equipment, work out what is actually wrong, and say so plainly.

Sometimes the answer is a part and the job ends there. Sometimes the honest answer is a new system. You hear which one it is before anything comes apart.

  1. 1

    You tell us what the unit is doing

    Warm air and no cooling. A noise that is not there any more. A breaker that trips. A thermostat that will not hold a set point. Photos of the unit help more than a description.

  2. 2

    We look at the system

    A rooftop unit gets looked at on the roof, where it lives. A split gets looked at inside and out, at the head and the outdoor side, because a cooling fault shows up in both places.

  3. 3

    You get the finding, then the work

    What is wrong with it, what it takes to put right, and what the system looks like afterwards. No selling on the doorstep and no changing the scope without telling you.

Four summer questions

What people ask
from a hot house.

01The air is running but it is blowing warm. What do I do?

Moving air that is not cold is a fault, not a setting. Call or text and tell us the outdoor unit is running and has been for a while. A refrigerant charge and a dirty coil or filter are the first two things we look at, and both can turn a cooling system into a fan that only moves hot air.

02The rooftop unit is louder than it used to be.

Rooftop units get loud. A blower wheel with a build-up of dust on it, a fan motor wearing out, or a loose panel will all do it, and on a gravel roof in Phoenix the noise carries a long way. Send a short recording if you can, or describe when it started and whether it changes with the thermostat.

03How do I book?

Call or text (623) 313-5657 and say what the system is doing and where it is. Rooftop package units need roof access, so if it is a three-storey building say so when you call, because it changes the ladder work and the time it takes.

04What should I send with the message?

A photo of the outdoor unit with the service panel number readable, one of the thermostat, and the make and model of anything changed in the last few years. If the air is blowing warm, add a photo of the filter and of where the condensate drains.
