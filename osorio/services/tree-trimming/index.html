Trees grow fast in the desert, and a dense, overgrown canopy is the first thing to give in a monsoon gust. Trimming isn't about making a tree smaller for its own sake. Good to know

Don't take too much at once

As a rule of thumb, arborists avoid removing more than about a quarter of a tree's canopy in one go. Over-pruning stresses a tree and can leave it weaker, not safer. If a tree needs more than that, it's usually done in stages.

Common questions

How often should trees be trimmed?
Most trees benefit from a look every year or two. Fast growers and trees close to a roof or wall may need it more often. Dense, top-heavy canopies are often trimmed before monsoon season (June 15 to September 30).
Can you trim a tree that touches power lines?
Trees growing into or touching utility lines are a job for the utility company or a certified line-clearance crew. Stay well clear and call the utility.
Do I need a permit to trim a tree in Mesa?
Routine trimming on your own property usually doesn't. Trees in the public right-of-way and some protected native trees can be different, so check with the City of Mesa if you're unsure.
What's the difference between trimming and pruning?
People use the words interchangeably. Pruning usually means removing specific branches for the tree's health and structure, and trimming often means shaping or reducing size. Either way, the right cuts in the right places matter more than the word.
