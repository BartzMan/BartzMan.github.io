One day in the valley

The sun climbs. The house keeps up.

The Tri-Valley swings from warm afternoons to cool nights. Tap a time on the curve and see which job matters then.

6 AM

Fresh air and exhaust fans

A bathroom exhaust fan pulls steam and stale air out of the house, so the morning starts dry and fresh.

When you need it
When the mirror stays fogged long after a shower, the fan is noisy, or it has stopped moving air.
What it is
A small ceiling fan that vents moist air outside instead of into the walls.
3 PM

Air conditioning at the peak

The condenser sits outside and moves heat out of your home, so the hottest hour of the day stays comfortable inside.

When you need it
When the house will not cool on a hot afternoon, the unit runs nonstop, or air from the vents feels weak.
What it is
The outdoor half of an air conditioner, paired with a coil and fan indoors.
8 PM

Heating for the cool-down

Once the sun drops behind the hills the valley cools fast, and a furnace warms the air that moves through the house.

When you need it
When rooms stay chilly on winter evenings, the furnace turns on and off often, or it makes new noises.
What it is
A furnace heats air and a blower sends it through the ducts and vents.
