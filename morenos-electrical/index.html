Circuit 04 · Follow the trace

From the pole to the panel.

Power comes into a building along one path. Tap a stop on the trace to see what that part is, and the signs that it needs an electrician.

Stop 1 of 5

The mast and service drop

The pipe on the outside wall that holds the wires coming from the pole and carries them down to the meter.

Signs: a mast leaning or pulled loose, wires sagging low or rubbing on the roof edge. Keep clear of it and call.

Stop 2 of 5

The meter

The utility's meter measures what the building uses. Its box, the meter can, is part of the building's own wiring.

Signs: a rusted or cracked meter can, a loose box on the wall, scorch marks around the glass.

Stop 3 of 5

The disconnect

A switch box that can cut all power to the building or to one big machine, so it can be worked on safely.

Signs: a handle that won't move, a box that hums or feels hot, a cover that no longer closes.

Stop 4 of 5

The panel

The breaker panel splits the power into circuits, one breaker for each, and trips a breaker when a circuit is overloaded.

Signs: breakers that trip again and again, no empty spaces left, buzzing, a warm door or a burning smell.

Stop 5 of 5

Circuits and fixtures

From the panel, each circuit runs to its outlets, switches and lights. This is where most remodel work happens.

Signs: flickering lights, outlets that are warm or loose, a switch that sparks. Turn that circuit off at the panel.
