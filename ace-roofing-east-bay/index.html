Roof types — pick your deck

Flat or pitched? The city wears both.

One block can carry a torch-down flat roof on one building and pitched shingle on the next. Tap your roof type to see how it is built and what it asks of you.

Your roof is

Torch-down flat roof

The whole roof is one waterproof sheet, torch-fused to the deck, sloped so rain finds a drain or a scupper. Watch the long seams and the low corners — that is where a flat roof first asks for help.

Shingle roof

Shingle sheds rain down its slopes, and the metal flashing carries the valleys and walls. Watch the tabs and the flashing lines — lifted edges are the first sign.

Two ways to fix a roof

TEAR-OFF

Some roofs get a new skin. Some go all the way down to the boards. We work both ways — and the photo below is the second one.

Repair — the surgical call

When the deck is sound and the wear is local, the fix stays local: open that one patch, rebuild it, blend the courses back in.

  • Lifted seams and open flashing
  • Cracked or slipped tiles
  • A leak that tracks to one spot

Replace — down to the boards

When the deck itself is tired, the roof comes down to the plywood, soft bays get rebuilt, and the system goes back new from the deck up.

  • Full tear-off to the boards
  • New decking at soft bays
  • Sheets, curbs and flashing re-laid in order

Torch-down and modified bitumen laid slope to drain, parapets closed in, scuppers and skylight curbs flashed into the plane.

Shingle & flashing

Shingle roofing is the overlapping courses that shed rain, plus the metal flashing at every valley, wall and vent.

Architectural shingle over underlayment, valleys flashed in metal, ridge lines vented — new roofs and single-course repairs alike.

Shingle courses, granular texture illustrative stock

03

Tile roofs

Tile roofing is heavy clay set over the deck; it is worked tile by tile, from the ridge down to the eave.

Tile lifted, stacked and reset over new underlayment — or stripped to the deck when the boards below have had enough.

Tile tear-off day on a stucco duplex — the old roof coming down by the bag

04

Skylights & openings

Skylight work is the framed curb and flashing that turns a hole in a roof into daylight without turning it into a leak.

Openings cut, curbs built and flashed into the roof plane — the deck rebuilt around them so the sheet runs clean to the frame.

Useful — from the ground, not the ladder

Before the rainy season.

San Francisco roofs get their real test in winter. Tap each check for what to look for — and never climb up to do it; that part is for a roofer.

Drains & scuppers

Flat roofs drain at their low corners. Leaves and debris block them quickly; if a puddle still sits two days after rain, the drain path is asking for attention.

Seams & flashing

On flat roofs watch the long seams and the metal at walls and skylights; on shingle, watch the valley lines. Lifted or dark-edged strips after rain are the first sign.

Shingle tabs

From the ground, missing or lifted tabs read as darker patches in the pattern. One patch is a repair; a scattered pattern is a longer conversation.

Ceiling stains

The roof talks indoors first: a stain that grows after a storm, or paint that bubbles on a ceiling line, is the roof leaving a note.
