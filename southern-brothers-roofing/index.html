Shingle styles

Pick a style, see the roof

Roofs in Waxahachie range from plain ranch to full Victorian. Tap a style to see a drawn swatch, a photo and the kind of house it suits.

Architectural

Thicker shingles built in layers, so the roof has depth and shadow instead of a flat look. It is the most common choice on homes today.

Where it suitsMost houses: brick ranches, two-storeys and newer builds.

Three-tab

Flat, even shingles cut into three tabs. The look is clean and simple, and it is a lighter style than architectural shingles.

Where it suitsPlain gables, small homes and outbuildings where a simple look fits.

Fish-scale accent

Shingles cut with rounded ends and laid in overlapping rows so the pattern looks like fish scales. It is often used as an accent in a gable or on a turret.

Where it suitsVictorian and Queen Anne style homes, turrets and porch gables.

Older homes

Old house, new roof

A roof on a 1900s house is not the same job as a roof on a new build. Tap each part to see what changes.

Older homes often have plank decking with small gaps instead of sheets of plywood. Any boards that are soft or damaged should be replaced or covered before new roofing goes on.

Older attics were often built with less airflow. Soffit and ridge vents let air move through, so the attic stays drier and cooler and the roof ages more evenly.

A thin metal strip along the eaves and rakes that guides water into the gutter instead of behind the fascia board. Many older roofs never had one.

Metal flashing seals the place where the roof meets a chimney, wall or dormer. Worn flashing is one of the most common sources of leaks in an older house.
