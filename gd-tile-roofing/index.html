No. 5 Glazed Materials

Clay, concrete
or shingle?

Clay tile

Fired clay in barrel or flat shapes, in terracotta and rose tones. It is heavy and brittle, which is why a roof like this is not walked on casually.

Concrete tile

Pressed from cement and sand, in flat or S shapes. It is a common tile type and needs the same care underfoot as clay.

Shingles and chimney flashing

Asphalt shingles lie flat in overlapping rows. Where a chimney rises through the roof, the metal flashing around its base needs to stay sealed.

No. 6 Charred The spotter

Tile roof in San Antonio? 5 things to look for from the ground.

Stand in the yard with a phone zoom or binoculars and tap each number on the drawn roof to see what that sign looks like. Never walk on tile: it cracks underfoot and it is slippery. If you spot any of these, photograph them from the ground and call a roofer.

Cracked or slipped tiles

A tile that sits crooked, has a visible crack or is missing leaves the layer beneath exposed.
