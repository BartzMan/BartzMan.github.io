Choose the roofline that looks like yours.

Composition shingle

Look for lifted edges, missing tabs and dark patches. A local repair may suit a small affected area if the surrounding shingles are still sound.

Concrete tile

Tiles shed water over the layer beneath them. A cracked tile or a leak near a roof junction can be a reason to check the underlayment and flashing.

Flat or low-slope

A low-slope roof depends on its continuous membrane and drainage. Persistent puddles, split seams or staining inside are worth having assessed.

Patch the spot
or plan a reroof?

Start with the condition of the surrounding roof. A single damaged area and widespread wear call for different conversations.

Isolated damage

Roof repair addresses a localized problem, such as a missing shingle or loose flashing, when the nearby roof is still sound.

Widespread wear

Roof replacement renews the covering when many areas are worn, leaks keep returning or damage extends beyond a small patch.

East Bay rainy-season checklist

Before the
rain comes.

Make a short note of any changes you see from the ground or inside the house.

Gutters

From the ground, look for leaf buildup at the roof edge or overflow marks on the siding.

Flashing

Look for obvious gaps around visible wall junctions, vents or chimneys. Note where you saw them.

Shingles

Use a ground-level view to spot raised edges or gaps in the roof covering. Take a photo if you can see the area clearly.

Attic stains

Where the attic is safely accessible, look for new discoloration on the underside of the roof or note any new ceiling stains indoors.
