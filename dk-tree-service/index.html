01The oak map

Where would the marks go?

An arborist marks a tree before a cut. Tap a numbered flag on this Houston live oak to see what that kind of job looks like and what it involves.

Plan sheet · Houston live oak
1

Deadwood

Dead limbs are bare, grey and brittle, with no leaves while the rest of the tree is in leaf. Dead wood can drop without warning.

What it involves

Cutting the dead limb back to the nearest healthy joint and lowering it to the ground.

2

Crossing limbs

Two limbs that rub where they cross wear through the bark and leave wounds that stay open to insects and rot.

What it involves

One of the pair is shortened or removed so the other can grow clear, leaving a clean cut at the collar.

3

Limbs over the roof

Branches that hang over shingles drop leaves, scrape in the wind and can come down on the house in a storm.

What it involves

A climber works the canopy back so there is open air above the roof, taking the limb in pieces rather than letting it drop.

4

Low limbs over the drive

Low limbs catch tall vehicles, block the view of the street and get in the way of the garbage truck.

What it involves

Lifting the canopy: the lowest limbs come off so there is clear height above the driveway and the curb.

5

The whole tree

When a tree is dead, split, leaning or too close to the house to keep, it comes down.

What it involves

The tree is taken down in sections, top first, each piece roped and lowered. The wood and brush are then cut up and hauled off.

6

The stump

A stump is what is left after a removal. It is a trip hazard, it takes up lawn and it can send up shoots.

What it involves

A grinder works the stump down below the surface so the ground can be filled and replanted.

02What gets cut

Trim it. Take it down. Grind it.

A

Trimming and shaping

Cutting back growth so a tree stays in proportion, off the roof and open to light. You need it when branches touch the house or the tree has grown lopsided.

B

Fine pruning

Careful, selective cuts that thin and tidy a tree’s structure. You need it when the tree is staying and you want it looking its best.

C

Tree removal

Taking a whole tree down, usually piece by piece near houses and fences. You need it when a tree is dead, leaning or in the wrong place.

D

Clear-up

Cut rounds and brush are cleared from the lawn, with a chipper on the truck for the small stuff.

E

Stump removal

Grinding a stump down below the surface of the lawn. You need it after a removal, or for an old stump that is in the way of the mower.
