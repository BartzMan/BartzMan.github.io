Selah.A pause to reflect.

A roof is the part of a building nobody looks at until it asks for attention. Good roof work starts the same way this word does: you stop, you look at the whole building, and then you decide what to do.

Churches, commercial buildings and family homes each have different roofs and different problems. The first step is always the same: look before you act.

What we roof

One roof at a time, for the building you care about.

01

New roofs for churches and commercial buildings

A full replacement is for when the old roof has run its course: the whole surface comes off or is covered with a new system, and the details are rebuilt around steeples, walls and vents.

02

Restoration

When a roof is worn but not finished, restoration repairs and renews what is there so the building keeps its roof for longer.

03

Waterproofing

Seams, joints and flat sections are sealed so water has nowhere to get in. You need it when stains appear below a flat roof.

01

Home roof replacement

When shingles are worn through, curling or leaking in several places, a new roof is usually cleaner than repair after repair.

02

Repair and restoration

Missing shingles, small leaks and worn patches can often be fixed without replacing the whole roof.

03

Waterproofing the details

Edges, flashing and joints are where water gets in first, so they get particular attention.

How a roof visit goes

Four quiet steps, no rush.

  1. Pause and look. Walk the whole roof and every wall, steeple and vent it touches, not just the spot that leaks.
  2. Listen. Where have stains or drips appeared, and after which storms? Photos from the building team help.
  3. Explain. Plain words and pictures of what was found, and whether it is a repair, a restoration or a new roof.
  4. Plan the week. A building used on weekends needs a schedule that works around its calendar.
