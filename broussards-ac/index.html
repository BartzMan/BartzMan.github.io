Storm-ready A/C

Gulf storm coming?

Before a storm or hurricane knocks the power out, protect the A/C. It’s the reminder Broussard’s posts every storm season. Here is what that means before, during and after.

Stage 1 · Before

Get the A/C out of harm’s way.

  1. Think about surge protection. Power coming back after an outage can spike. A surge protector for the A/C is worth asking about well before the season.
  2. Clear around the condenser. Move patio chairs, pots and loose branches that wind could throw into the outdoor unit.
  3. Know your breakers. Find the A/C breaker in the panel and the disconnect box by the outdoor unit now, while it’s dry and light out.

Stage 2 · During

When the lights start to flicker.

  1. Set the thermostat to Off. No point in the system trying to start on unsteady power.
  2. Switch the A/C breaker off. If it is safe to reach the panel, turning it off keeps the system out of the surge when power returns.
  3. Hold the cool in. Keep doors shut and blinds drawn so the house stays cooler for longer.

Stage 3 · After

Look first, then restart.

  1. Check the unit from the ground. Look for branches, debris or bent panels on and around the outdoor unit. Clear what is loose.
  2. Water reached it? Leave it off. A unit that sat in standing water needs checking by a pro before it runs again.
  3. Wait for steady power. When the lights have stayed on, switch the breaker back on, then set the thermostat to Cool.

General advice for any home. If anything smells of burning or the breaker trips again, leave it off and call a pro.

Before you call

6Houston A/C not cooling? 6 checks before you call

Six things any homeowner can look at in ten minutes, without tools and without a ladder. Tap each one as you go.

0 of 6 checked

  1. Pull the return-air filter. If it’s gray and matted, swap it: a clogged filter chokes the airflow and the house stays warm.

  2. Look for a tripped A/C breaker in the panel. Reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off: that one is for a pro.

  3. Is it set to Cool, a few degrees below the room? A blank screen can just mean dead batteries.

  4. Outside, is the fan on top spinning? From the ground, clear leaves, clippings and plants away from the coil.

  5. A full drain pan can trip a float switch that shuts the system off. Look for standing water at the indoor unit.

  6. Ice on the copper lines? Switch cooling off and run just the fan to thaw it. Don’t chip at the ice.

  7. All six checked and still warm? That’s the moment to call. Tell us which checks you did; it saves a step.
