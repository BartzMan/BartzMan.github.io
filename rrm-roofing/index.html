Step 1 · the sea wall

Tap a numbered part of the house. In plain words: how salt air, Gulf humidity and storm wind wear on it, and what a roofer looks at there.

What salt air and wind do

Gulf humidity and salt in the air feed algae streaks and wear the gritty surface away faster, and a hard wind can lift the edges of a shingle until it creases or tears loose.

What a roofer checks

Curling or cupped edges, bare patches, cracked or missing tabs, and loose grit collecting in the gutters.

What salt air and wind do

The ridge is the highest line on the roof, so it takes the full push of a bay wind. Cap pieces can loosen and open a path for wind-driven rain.

What a roofer checks

That the cap shingles are sealed and straight along the whole ridge, and that nothing along the line has lifted.

What salt air and wind do

Flashing is the metal that turns water away at chimneys, walls, valleys and edges. Salt air corrodes metal over time, and sealant dries out in the Texas sun.

What a roofer checks

Rust or pitting, edges that have lifted, gaps in the sealant, and any place water could track behind the metal.

What salt air and wind do

Exposed nail heads can rust in salty, humid air and then back out, leaving small holes that let rain in. Wind pulls hardest on fasteners that were not seated well.

What a roofer checks

Rusted or popped nails, nails sitting proud of the surface, and sealant over exposed heads that has cracked.

What salt air and wind do

Every vent and pipe is a hole through the roof with a rubber or metal collar around it. Sun and salt air crack those collars, and storm wind drives rain right at them.

What a roofer checks

Cracked rubber collars, loose vent caps, rust at the base, and any stain on the ceiling below a vent.

What salt air and wind do

Sand, leaves and seed pods collect in gutters, and a Gulf downpour then spills over the edge instead of leaving the roof, soaking the fascia and the ground by the wall.

What a roofer checks

Sagging runs, loose hangers, clogged downspouts, and whether water is carried away from the house.

What salt air and wind do

The underside of the overhang breathes for the attic. Wind pushes rain up into it, and humid air leaves stains, peeling paint and soft wood that show up there first.

What a roofer checks

Stains, peeling paint, soft or sagging boards, and whether the vent openings are clear.

Step 2 · the work

Services

Pick the job closest to yours. Not sure which it is? Call and describe what you see.

Three steps down to the water.

Roof repair

Fixing a leak, a patch of lifted or missing shingles, or flashing that has let go, without replacing the whole roof.

When: the damage is in one area and the rest of the roof still looks sound.

Storm and wind damage repairs

Putting right what a blow leaves behind: torn-up shingle tabs, debris damage, loosened ridge caps.

When: after a storm you can see damage from the ground, or find a stain inside.

Roof replacement

Taking the old roof off down to the deck and putting a new one on.

When: damage is spread across the roof, leaks keep coming back, or the surface is worn through.

Construction and exterior repairs

Carpentry and exterior work around the roof, such as decking, fascia and framing.

When: a roof problem has reached the wood underneath, or you need exterior work done alongside it.
