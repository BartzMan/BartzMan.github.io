The five coats · drag the trowel

From cracked to finished.

Most prewar walls in upper Manhattan need more than paint. Slide through the five stages a wall goes through, and see what each one fixes, and what shows if it’s skipped.

Stage 1 · Cracked plaster

Stage 1 of five

Cracked plaster

What happens here
Loose and hollow plaster is cut back or secured, and cracks are opened slightly so the filler has something to grip.
Skip it and you’ll see
Cracks come straight back through the new paint, often along the same lines.

Three finishes · from bare wall to last coat

Paint, plaster & sheetrock.

Finish 01

Painting

Walls, ceilings, trim, doors and closets, cut in by brush and rolled out even: what you need when a room looks tired, a colour is changing, or an apartment turns over between tenants.

  • Walls & ceilingsPrepped, primed where needed, then finish coats.
  • Trim, doors & closetsCrisp edges on casings, baseboards and shelving.
  • Colour changesDark to light, accent walls or a whole apartment.
Ask about painting

Finish 02

Plaster & skim coat

Plastering repairs cracked, hollow or broken plaster, and a skim coat is a thin layer troweled over the whole wall: what you need when prewar walls are cracked, patched or wavy and paint alone won’t hide it.

  • Crack & hole repairCut back, taped, filled and sanded flush.
  • Full-wall skim coatingOne flat, even plane before primer and paint.
  • Prewar ceilings & cornersCracked ceilings, outside corners and old patches.
Ask about plaster

Finish 03

Sheetrock & taping

Sheetrock is the board used for new walls, ceilings and closets, and taping is the tape-and-compound work that hides its seams: what you need for a new partition, a closed-in closet or a ceiling replaced after a leak.

  • New walls & ceilingsPartitions, closets and soffits hung on studs.
  • Taping & finishing seamsTape, compound and sanding until the seams disappear.
  • Patches after other tradesHoles left by plumbing or electrical work closed up.
Ask about sheetrock
