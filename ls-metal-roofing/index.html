Panel 02 · Metal in Pasadena

Why metal makes sense on the Gulf Coast.

Pasadena sits in some of the hardest weather in Texas: months of heat, sideways rain, salt air off the ship channel and wind that finds every loose edge. A properly fastened metal roof is built for exactly that.

  • Heat
    Reflective panels

    Light-colored and coated metal reflects more of the summer sun than dark shingles, which helps the attic stay cooler.

  • Wind
    Locked seams and screw patterns

    Seams and fastener spacing are set for Gulf Coast wind, with edge and ridge details that keep panels from lifting.

  • Rain
    Long panels, fewer joints

    Panels run from ridge to eave in one piece, so there are far fewer places for water to get in than with shingles.

  • Salt
    Coated steel for salty air

    Galvalume and painted panels are chosen to hold up against the humid, salty air near the channel.

Panel 03 · Process

How a metal roof goes on.

Four plain steps. You hear what we found and what we plan to do at each one, before we move to the next.

  1. 1

    Walk the roof

    We get on the roof, check the decking, penetrations and existing flashing, and take photos to show you.

  2. 2

    Pick the panel

    Standing seam or R-panel, gauge, color and trim. We measure and order panels cut to your roof's lengths.

  3. 3

    Tear off and dry in

    Old material comes off, bad decking gets replaced, and underlayment goes down so the deck is dried in before any panel is set.

  4. 4

    Panels, trim, clean-up

    Panels, ridge, flashing and trim go on, screws are checked, and the yard is run over with a magnet for stray fasteners.

Four common metal panel profiles and the roof shapes they go on. Not sure which one you have, or which one you want? Send a photo and ask.

Standing seam

Concealed fasteners. Raised seams lock the panels together, so no screw heads face the weather.

R-panel / PBR

Exposed fasteners. Tall trapezoid ribs that cover long runs on shops, barns and carports.
